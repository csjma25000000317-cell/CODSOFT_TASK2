KNOW THE WARNING SIGNS

How to spot a phishing attempt

Attackers often use urgency, impersonation and misleading links to influence decisions.

✉

Suspicious emails

Look for unexpected requests, mismatched sender addresses, unusual attachments and pressure to act quickly.

🔗

Misleading URLs

Check the actual hostname carefully. Misspellings, unfamiliar domains and unexpected subdomains can be warning signs.

🔐

Fake login pages

A familiar logo does not prove authenticity. Reach important accounts through their official app or known website.

🎭

Social engineering

Scammers may pretend to be a bank, recruiter, delivery service or trusted person to gain your confidence.

Quick scenario

A message says your account will be suspended unless you sign in immediately.

Do not follow the message link. Visit the official service independently and check your account there.

UNDERSTAND THE TACTICS

Common social engineering techniques

Urgency

“Act now or your account will be closed.”

Pause and verify.
Impersonation

A message pretends to come from a trusted organization.

Verify the sender independently.
Curiosity

An unexpected attachment promises shocking or exciting content.

Do not open unverified files.
Reward or fear

A prize, refund or threat is used to influence your decision.

Check through official channels.
LEARN FROM SCENARIOS

Phishing case studies

These are illustrative educational scenarios, not claims about verified individual incidents.

CASE 01EMAIL

Fake account alert

Scenario: An email claims an account will be closed unless the recipient signs in immediately.

Warning signs: Unexpected urgency, an unfamiliar sender and a login link.

Safe response: Open the official app or website independently and check the account.

CASE 02DELIVERY

Unexpected delivery fee

Scenario: A text claims a parcel is waiting for a small extra payment.

Warning signs: Unexpected payment request and an unverified tracking link.

Safe response: Verify the tracking number through the courier's official service.

CASE 03RECRUITMENT

Fake job offer

Scenario: A recruiter promises guaranteed work but requests an unexpected fee.

Warning signs: Unverifiable recruiter details, pressure and payment demands.

Safe response: Independently verify the employer and never rush into payment.
